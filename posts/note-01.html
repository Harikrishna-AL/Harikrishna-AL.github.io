Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting with Generative Replay

Abstract: This note investigates the use of a dual-model architecture (Generator + Solver) to pseudo-rehearse previous tasks without storing original data.
1. The Concept

In a standard continual learning setup, a model trains on Task $T_1$, then $T_2$. When training on $T_2$, the gradients optimize the loss $\mathcal{L}_{T2}$ but often increase $\mathcal{L}_{T1}$ (forgetting).

Deep Generative Replay (DGR) proposes training a generator $G$ alongside the solver $S$. The generator learns the distribution of the input data $P(x)$.

2. Mathematical Formulation

The total loss function becomes a mix of the new data and the generated "replay" data:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_{new}(x, y) + (1-\alpha) \mathcal{L}_{replay}(G(z), S(G(z))) $$

Where $G(z)$ produces a "fake" sample from the previous task's distribution.
